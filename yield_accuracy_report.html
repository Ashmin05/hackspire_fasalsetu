Benchmark vs Accuracy Report · West Bengal pilot (district level)

Generated 03 October 2026 · Benchmark: Directorate of Economics & Statistics, Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare, Govt. of India · 1,753 official district records (1997-98 to 2022-23)

1. Summary

FasalSetu estimates the yield of Aman rice, Boro rice, potato and wheat for every West Bengal district from what the Sentinel-2 satellite sees during the season (crop greenness), the season's rainfall and temperature. To prove the estimates can be trusted, every one was checked against the official Government of India yield for a season the model had never seen, and compared with three ways of guessing that use no satellite at all.

2. The benchmark (the "answer key")

Official district Area-Production-Yield statistics published by the Directorate of Economics & Statistics, Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare, Govt. of India. This is the government's own record of how much each district harvested, collected through crop-cutting experiments — the same numbers used for official agricultural planning. We did not produce or adjust them.

  • Source files: DES crop-wise APY (1997-98 to 2022-23). Provenance and download details: backend/data/benchmarks/README.md.
  • Yield recomputed as production ÷ area; rows under 1,000 ha sown or with impossible yields are excluded.
  • Crop mapping: Aman = Rice/Winter, Boro = Rice/Summer, Potato = Potato/Rabi, Wheat = Wheat/Rabi.

How a prediction is made
Predicted yield = trend × (1 + satellite & weather correction)
• Trend: a straight line through the district's own official yields of the previous 12 years (better seeds, irrigation, practices).
• Correction: a ridge regression that turns this season's differences from the district's usual greenness, rain and heat into a % change in yield — a greener-than-usual season pushes the estimate up; a drought, heat wave or cyclone pulls it down.

4. How we tested — and why it is fair

  1. Hide one season (leave-one-year-out). Train on every other season; the hidden season's official yield is never seen.
  2. Predict it for every district, then compare with the official yield. Repeat for every season.
  3. Baselines are scored the same way, using only official yields from earlier years: (a) same as last year, (b) average of the last 5 years, (c) the 12-year trend line.
  4. Feature anomalies ("greener than usual") are measured against the training seasons only.
  5. The model must earn its place: if it doesn't beat the best of the three baselines for a crop, the app serves that baseline instead and says so (the same rule FasalSetu's price forecaster follows).
  6. All of the above is enforced by automated tests (backend/tests/test_yield_model.py) — e.g. a test proves that changing a hidden season's official yield cannot change its prediction. Result: 24 passed in 47.25s.

Limitations (stated openly)

  • District level. Official yields are only published per district, so accuracy is proven at district scale. A single farm can do better or worse than its district; the app labels farm estimates as district estimates with a range.
  • Short Sentinel-2 record. Sentinel-2 imagery over West Bengal starts in 2016, so 7–7 seasons per crop could be scored against the benchmark. Each new year of official figures adds one more test season with no code change (e.g. 2023-24, see the benchmark README).
  • Mixed cropland. Greenness is averaged over all cropland in the district during the season window; it is not separated by crop type (e.g. Boro rice and potato overlap in Jan–Mar).
  • Design history. The first version read winter-crop greenness over all cropland. After the first evaluation the Boro, potato and wheat features were switched to crop-specific masks (flooded paddy for Boro; non-flooded winter fields for potato and wheat, each in its own peak-growth window). That revision did not change the verdict for those crops. Because the design was revised after seeing held-out results, the next official season (2023-24) is the first fully untouched check.
  • Recent seasons are true forecasts. Estimates after 2022-23 cannot be scored yet; they will be as official figures are released.

python scripts/yield_accuracy_report.py     # this report
